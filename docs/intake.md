# Intake: untagged PDFs

`intake.py` runs first in every run (`INTAKE=off` turns it off). A tagged PDF is left alone ("none (already tagged)"). **Placeholder tag trees (10.5):** a tag tree counts only when it has at least one element that carries text structure (P, H1–H6, lists, tables, Span, Note, Link, Caption and the like, after the RoleMap). A tree with only grouping elements (Document, Part, Sect, Div, Art) or only Figure tags is a placeholder: on the sponsors' 148 books, 25 of the 59 "tagged" ones (Acrobat Distiller/APL 8.0 books with one Part per page; Prince books with Figure tags only). Intake records it in `intake.json` (`tag_tree`: the elements, MarkInfo, and every Figure's alt text with its page, MCIDs and image box; alt whose whole text, trimmed and in any case, says nothing ("Decorative element" as Prince writes it, "image", "image N", "figure", "figure N", "graphic", "picture", "photo", "untitled") is recorded as `placeholder`, anything else (such as "Photo of …") as `publisher`), removes it from its working copy only (the original is unchanged; a check confirms pages and text did not change) and handles the book as untagged. Publisher alt text is recorded, not yet carried into `intake.pdf` or passed to the AI (a CHECK says so). MarkInfo does not decide it: 12 of the 148 have full trees with MarkInfo missing or Marked false, as do three regression books (pearl, performing, translation), and they stay "already tagged". An earlier "skipped" intake is reused only if the tree still counts as real (`FROM=` reruns always reuse). For an untagged one, everything is local, no model writes or changes any text, and the original is never written to. The sponsor-checklist step each part serves is in brackets.

1. **Triage.** Every page is classed: visible text, image only (no text, and images covering at least 25% of the visible page area together: a scan or a picture page such as a Part opener), an existing invisible OCR layer, or blank. A page whose text operators draw no character (an empty text object over a scan, as in two ScholarSpace downloads) is classed by its images alone, so a scan is OCR'd; the pages are listed as a note (`triage.empty_text_pages`). A page counts as empty only when pdfplumber also finds no character on it (PDFium misses most of Acrobat's hidden vertical OCR text). Before OCR, intake removes those empty text objects from its working copy (OCRmyPDF passes over any page with a text object, even an empty one); a check confirms nothing visible or in the text changed (`checks.empty_text_removed`). **Private-use characters (3.6):** every text page is checked for characters with private-use codes (legacy fonts use them for e.g. U+F730–F739 old-style digits, U+F761–F77A small capitals), which a screen reader may read as nothing or nonsense. Their count, pages and code ranges are recorded in `intake.json` (`private_use`); more than 100 is a CHECK. **Text outside the visible page area:** characters that lie outside the CropBox (e.g. a printer's slug in the margin) are invisible on screen and in print but read by a screen reader unless marked as artifacts; their count and pages are recorded (`outside_page_area`) and flagged. **Word breaks (3.4):** for every text page, PDFium tells real space characters from spaces it makes up from gaps between letters. A book where most word breaks are gaps only gets a note with the share of pages (`word_breaks`; Prince and TeX books are typically over 90%, Distiller books near 0%). A gap is **narrow** when it is under 19% of the type size (font size × text matrix), measured between advance widths, which is what a text extractor uses. The cut was calibrated on two Acrobat copies: on bringing PDF 86 Acrobat ran words together at 14.9–18.3% ("concernedwithChinese", "theWestern", "theWest"), and on 9798880703272 PDF 9 it spaced 19.8% correctly. The book is judged as a whole: when narrow breaks average **10 or more per text page** it is a **CHECK** listing the 20 worst pages; below that, a note with the rate. Detection only: nothing is changed.
   **Two printed pages per PDF page (4.1, 4.2):** a page that, as displayed (after its /Rotate), is landscape (width at least 1.15 × height) with a gutter in the middle 40–60% of its width and 25% or more of the ink on each side is a spread; a landscape page of the same size next to one counts too (a chapter end leaves a half blank). Calibrated: mohr-zen-1995 17 of 19 pages, reynolds-magazines-1989 10 of 10; no false alarm in 178 other books. Pages are never split. A CHECK lists them (`spreads`). Where the top or bottom band of each half shows a pair of page numbers that differ by one, and a neighbouring spread's pair is 2 higher or lower (so years in the running heads, which do not rise, are not taken), both are kept (`spreads.printed`) and the page is cited as "PDF 3 (printed 210–211)" in intake.md and review.md (a range: "printed 208–209 to 218–219"); numbers are never guessed.
2. **Is the text layer usable? (1.2)** Up to 20 pages with text, spread through the book (in a book with 40 or more text pages, not from the first 5% of them, at least 6: front matter such as the copyright page is set in small type that OCR reads poorly), plus up to 4 that look suspicious (private-use characters, very long run-together words) are drawn at 300 dpi and read by Tesseract, and the OCR text is compared with the page's own text layer, after normalising quotes, dashes, case, spacing and line-end hyphens (CJK characters and underscores, which OCR never reads as fill-in rules, are left out). The sample pages are read with `eng` plus the packs the text layer calls for: kana `jpn`, hangul `kor`, 50 or more Vietnamese letters `vie` (`language.score_lang`; these never decide the OCR language). Only text inside the visible page area (CropBox) is compared.
   - **Page score:** the lower of the in-order character error rate and the any-order one (the same characters in any order), so a page whose text is right but read in a different order is not counted as wrong; it shows as "usable (reading order differs)". Both raw rates are recorded per page (`cer_in_order`, `cer_any_order`; `cer` is the page score).
   - **Book score:** the median of the page scores (`median_cer`). ≤ 5%: usable. Over 15%: unusable. In between: doubtful (the book goes on with a CHECK). Fewer than 5 pages scored: "not enough pages scored" (a CHECK).
   - **Pages that are not scored** (listed as "not scored" with the reason, left out of the median): images cover 25% or more of the page together; fewer than 300 characters in the text layer after normalising; or 20+ CJK characters (or 10%+ of its letters) when the OCR language has no CJK pack; **math**: a math font (CMMI, CMSY, CMEX, MSAM, MSBM, Symbol, MT Extra, "Math" …) and 10 or more math symbols or Greek letters in the text layer (20 or more without a math font), since OCR reads ∨ ∧ ≥ ∈ as v a > e; and **diacritics OCR did not read**: the text layer has 20 or more marked letters, 1.5 times as many as OCR read, and without the marks the page scores usable (pinyin tone marks with `eng`, for example). Those pages are listed, never passed: as a note on born-digital text (the OCR missed the marks), as a CHECK on an existing OCR layer (the layer itself may lack them). A text layer that lacks marks the print has still scores badly. Each is replaced by the nearest scorable page, so up to 20 pages are scored. How many pages in the whole book fall under each reason is recorded (`unscorable_in_book`).
   - **Unusable, but born-digital or math:** when the producer writes text (pdfTeX, XeTeX, LuaTeX, Prince, Microsoft Word, Adobe PDF Library, InDesign) or the book has math, an unusable verdict stops the book for a person without suggesting `FORCE_OCR=1` (OCR may simply not read the text); the doubtful message says the same.
   - **Flags:** sample pages that are unusable although the book is not; doubtful sample pages, as one CHECK listing them; pages with clearly fewer word breaks than the printed page ("words run together", 3.4).
   - **An existing OCR layer on scanned pages (1.2, 3.1, 3.7)** is not scored by the test above (its pages are images). Up to 10 such pages (spread through the book, past the front matter) are read again by a *different* Tesseract model for the layer's script (`script/Latin`, `script/Japanese_vert+script/Japanese`, `script/Hangul…`, `script/HanS…+script/HanT…`), since the book's own pack tends to share the layer's errors. The layer is read with pdfplumber when PDFium sees under half its characters (Acrobat's hidden vertical OCR text). Median error over 15%: a CHECK "OCR layer" (5–15%: the worse pages listed); no page scored at all (e.g. no script model installed): a CHECK "OCR layer not checked"; Latin pages with 10 or more math symbols are not scored. **Diacritics:** on pages whose letters agree (error without the marks 15% or less), the model reads 5 or more marked vowels a page on average and the layer has under 10% as many: a CHECK "OCR layer: diacritics" (henrycukim: ŏ/ŭ lost as o/u; 82 marked vowels read on 10 pages, 0 in the layer); such a page is judged on its letters. Never a stop, and the layer is never replaced (it may be the library's record). A flagged layer (doubtful, unusable or missing diacritics) makes the OCR text approval part of "Ready to ship", as added OCR text does: the row "OCR text reviewed by a person" stays CHECK until `review_ocr.py` (or the review page) records "approved". Recorded as `ocr_layer_test`. Calibrated on five books (henrycukim, gelfand, liouville, reynolds-magazines part 1, mohr): a starting point. Math scans score badly (gelfand 21%, liouville 27%) because their OCR'd math is junk letters, not math symbols.
3. **Language (3.7).** OCR uses `eng` unless `OCR_LANG` says otherwise; the scripts in the text layer and Tesseract's script detection on image-only pages (its names "Korean" and "Hangul", "Japanese", "Han" are read alike) are recorded and choose packs only for the 1.2 sample pages. **CJK on pages to be OCR'd:** before OCR, every planned page is read once with the Chinese, Japanese and Korean packs (with more than 12 pages to OCR, where this takes 18–60 s a page on a newspaper scan: without a CJK pack in `OCR_LANG` the check stops at the first CJK page, since the book stops either way, and the message says how many pages were checked; with one, 8 pages spread through the book are read, and a full run lists the CJK pages from the OCR text itself (intake.md's "CJK on pages to be OCR'd" row too); when no CJK is found, every page is still read, so a CJK glossary in an English scan is not missed. ktimes-2005-06, 54 pages, plan-only: about 20 minutes before, now 38 s without `OCR_LANG` and 5 minutes with `kor+eng`); 10 or more CJK characters at 60% confidence or more mean the page holds CJK text (calibrated: sojourners' glossary 47–229, every cover and Part opener 0–1). Then the book **stops** unless `OCR_LANG` names a CJK language, for Chinese, Japanese and Korean alike (it is never guessed; before 2026-10-24 a Japanese scan went on with `eng+jpn` chosen by intake, which on vertical text gave junk). **Vertical text:** vertical CJK reads poorly with the horizontal packs, so on pages where they find only 5–9 confident CJK characters, up to 3 of those pages (spread through the book) are read again with the installed `_vert` packs; when one of them finds 10 or more, all such pages count as CJK. The `_vert` packs read at most 3 pages in all: those, then the CJK pages with most CJK as a trial; when they read 1.5 times the confident CJK characters on most trial pages, the stop message suggests adding the `_vert` pack (e.g. `OCR_LANG=jpn_vert+jpn+eng`), and with an `OCR_LANG` that has none, a CHECK says so (`cjk_probe.vertical_trial`). With a CJK language set, the pages carry a CHECK "CJK OCR: needs a person" (CJK OCR is often wrong, and multi-column CJK lists may be read across the columns). Chinese characters with no kana are never guessed as Chinese or Japanese. With `FORCE_OCR=1` (no CJK probe), CJK text found in the book and no CJK pack in `OCR_LANG` flags the pages that get OCR text (a CHECK only when OCR is planned or kept; the scripts found are always recorded). A language with no installed pack is flagged.
4. **OCR (2.1, 3.1, 10.3, 10.4).** OCRmyPDF 17 with `--mode skip --pages <image-only pages> --output-type pdf --optimize 0 --oversample 300`: only pages with no text are OCR'd; pages with text are copied untouched; images are not recompressed and the file is not converted to PDF/A; scans under 300 dpi are read enlarged (the page image itself is unchanged) and flagged (in `--plan-only` as a note, since the run itself flags only the pages that keep OCR text). An image-only page whose OCR text is not text-like (fewer than 15 word-like tokens, or under 60% of its tokens) gets no OCR text: it is a picture (a cover, a Part opener) and goes to the figure step for alt text. Characters the OCR font could not map are counted (3.6). **Junk check:** on each page that keeps OCR text, tokens with symbols (# @ ® & …), capital clusters with no vowel ("MBH") or letters mixed with digits are counted; 5% or more of 30+ tokens is a CHECK "OCR text looks like junk" (calibrated: clean OCR 0%, sojourners' glossary 3.8–10.9% with `chi_tra+eng`).
   - **Unusable text layer:** the book **stops**, flagged, unless `FORCE_OCR=1` is set; then every page is OCR'd with `--mode force` (each page becomes an image, 400 dpi in our tests, with new invisible OCR text; the publisher's text layer is replaced, which review.md marks as a CHECK). **`FORCE_OCR=1` is unverified:** on synthetic test files OCRmyPDF redrew each page at 400 dpi, as a 1-bit black-and-white image when the page held only black text and as an 8-bit RGB image when it held a grey or colour picture. Either way 0.31–0.39% of each softened page differed and the after-OCR check (limit 0.3%) stopped the book. Until this is resolved, a book with an unusable text layer goes to a person.
5. **Auto-tag (4–7, 10.5).** OpenDataLoader (`--format tagged-pdf`, local mode, no hybrid server) adds a tag tree: headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, figures, captions, links; running heads and page numbers become artifacts. When there is OCR text, its hidden-text filter is turned off so the invisible OCR text is tagged too. On OCR'd pages, a Figure that is only the whole-page scan image is then marked as decoration: it is the page itself, not a figure (5.1). **Spreads (6.1–6.4):** the tagger's own order (xycut, its default; it has no per-half option) interleaves the two halves of a spread (mohr: 7–90 switches between halves a page). On spread pages only, intake reorders the tagger's elements by half: the right half first when the text is vertical CJK (an Identity-V font such as Acrobat's HiddenVertOCR, the vertical-text trial, or a `_vert` pack in `OCR_LANG`), with its columns from right to left; otherwise the left half first, keeping the tagger's order within a half. Only the tree order changes (checked: no page appearance or text change); per page the switches between halves before and after and whether the order changed are recorded, with the pages left in the tagger's order and why (a tag tree that is not one flat list under Document, or fewer than 2 elements on the page) (`checks.spreads_reordered`; mohr: 7–90 switches a page before, 1 after). The CHECK says what was done, not what was planned: in vertical text the body columns run right to left by position, and running heads, page numbers, ruby and stray OCR fragments go where their position falls (on mohr PDF 3 the running head of printed page 210 is the 14th element of its half). **Placeholder alt text (8.2, 2.4.4):** OpenDataLoader writes alt text on every Figure ("image 12") and every Link ("Annotation"). A screen reader would say those words instead of describing the figure or reading the link's own text, and veraPDF's alt-text rule passes on them. Intake removes alt text that is exactly one of those two forms (any other alt, such as a link's web address, is kept), and the count is a note in `intake.md` (`checks.placeholder_alt_removed` in `intake.json`). The figures then count as having no alt text: the AI writes it for those it is sent, and the rest are listed for a person (in dwellings, 443 of 543). verify's "Figures: none with empty or placeholder alt" row also counts alt such as "image 12" as a placeholder.
6. **Checks (a failed check stops the book, with the reason in `intake.json`):** after OCR, every page looks exactly as before and only the planned pages gained text (force OCR: pages may differ only by anti-aliasing); after auto-tagging, every page looks the same (a tolerance of 0.05% of a softened 72-dpi page allows sub-pixel glyph shifts where the tagger splits a text run; a page over it gets a second look at 300 dpi and passes when no quarter-inch tile has more than 50 changed pixels, which a missing character exceeds) and its text is unchanged (a page whose text holds exactly the same characters in a different order passes as "reordered": the tagger re-sequenced text runs; the moved fragments are listed in `intake.json` and review.md, and the tag tree sets the reading order); a tag tree exists; bookmarks, links and page labels are all still there. How much text the tag tree reaches is recorded.

The result, `_intake/intake.pdf`, is what audit, decide, apply and verify then work on; `verify.py` compares the remediated PDF with it, and intake's own checks compare it with the original. A rerun with the same settings reuses an earlier intake; `FROM=` reruns copy the earlier book's intake so a saved reply still fits the same file.

**Checks on every book (verify.py, checklist 3.6):** characters a screen reader reaches that have no Unicode value (`(cid:N)`, U+FFFD, control codes), and characters with private-use codes, with their code ranges; each is a CHECK when above 0.

**In review.md:** an "Intake:" line under the header; check rows for the text-layer test, an existing OCR layer ("Existing OCR layer agrees with a fresh OCR reading": CHECK when doubtful, unusable or diacritics are missing), spreads ("One printed page per PDF page": CHECK, with how many pages were reordered), word breaks, the OCR (with any low-resolution, language, unmapped-character or left empty-text issue) and **"OCR text reviewed by a person"**, which stays CHECK until someone records "approved" with `review_ocr.py` (`record.json` → `intake.review.ocr_text_approved`: verdict, reviewer, date, reason, pages; checklist rule 2; the review page's planned OCR section will write the same record, and `review_ocr.py` stays as the fallback: see [Sign-off](review-step.md#sign-off-when-a-book-is-ready-to-ship)); and an "Intake: OCR and auto-tagging" section with the per-page text-layer scores (a page that was not scored shows why, and which page replaced it), the OCR'd pages and every flag.

**Not done (yet):** OpenDataLoader's hybrid mode (a second, Docling-based server, which may handle complex tables better: 5.6–5.8), deskew/despeckle of degraded scans (2.2), re-reading low-confidence OCR lines (3.2), personal-information flagging (1.1), and PDF 2.0 books (stopped for a person: see [Not supported yet: PDF 2.0](troubleshooting.md#not-supported-yet-pdf-20)); veraPDF (11.2) is done (see [Verification with veraPDF](verapdf.md)). Accuracy of the OCR itself (CER/WER) needs a hand-corrected reference (11.1); the first candidate is `9780824882402.pdf`, PDF 381–386. Thresholds (5% / 15%, 15 words / 60%, the 25% image and 300-character rules, 5 pages, 100 private-use characters) are named constants at the top of `intake.py` and recorded in every `intake.json` (`settings.thresholds`); they are starting points to tune on real scores.

To see what intake would do to a folder of books without changing anything:

```bash
for f in ../samples/untagged/*.pdf; do python3 intake.py "$f" "/tmp/intake-plan/$(basename "$f" .pdf)" --plan-only; done
```
