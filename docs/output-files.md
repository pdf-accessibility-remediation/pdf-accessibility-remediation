# What a run leaves in `PDFREM/runs/<name>/<run-label>/`

| File | For | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `<original>_remediated.pdf` | everyone | The result. Run PAC 2026 on this (named by `OUT` or the book list if you set one) |
| `review.md` | the human reviewer | Automated checks (including exactly one H1, bookmarks present, every decision applied, nothing deferred: a green row is evidence, not proof); every alt text the AI wrote (before and after) and the ones it left alone; heading levels and spoken heading text; what was hidden as decoration; reading-order moves; what the AI and the executor deferred; anything not applied |
| `review.html` | the human reviewer | The main view for people, beside `review.md` (the record, which links to it). "Ready to ship?"; four status cards (safety, with its five checks in a fold that opens, failed check first, when one fails; needs a person; AI decisions; veraPDF before → after); a folded "Run details" (result file, intake, libraries, time); what needs a person, every description in full, with its source and pages and a "Details" link that opens the folded section at that very item (without JavaScript it lands on the section); a filter by source whose options show how many issues come from each ("All sources (13)", "Ship condition (1)"; not shown when all come from one source); a strip of the book's pages (one cell per page, shaded by its most serious issue; one Tab stop, arrow keys move, the page's issues are read out) and the same as a plain list; and every section of review.md, collapsible. Every page reference ("PDF 152 (printed 143)", as a whole word only: not "veraPDF 1.30.3" or "PDF 2.0") links to that page in the page strip and pins it there, showing what needs a person on it and every section that mentions it, each linked to the exact item. In the strip, hover and the arrow keys preview a page; a click or Enter pins it (a solid outline, with a matching swatch before its details; a preview has a dotted outline; "Pinned" and "Preview" are read out to screen readers, and pinning is announced), and while one is pinned a preview shows on its own line below it; clicking the pinned cell, clicking outside the strip, or Escape unpins it. A "§N Details" link opens the folded section at the very item and outlines it in blue until the next click elsewhere (one at a time). summary.html keeps its bars. The veraPDF section says, in one sentence, how many WCAG rules failed before by who can act on them (the AI, the pipeline, only a person) and how many after; review.md has it too. A section title that ends in a count equal to its badge is shown without it in review.html (review.md keeps it). Section badges count items (table rows and list items); a section with items that need a person also shows their number in an amber pill (read out as "N need(s) a person"), and each of those items has an amber dot (in a list it takes the bullet's place; in a table it comes before the first cell's text; read out as "Needs a person"); a key above the sections reads "● needs a person". They follow the Overview's definition exactly (automated checks marked CHECK, other than "Nothing deferred by the executor"; every deferral by the AI; the executor's deferrals by kind and page), so the numbers add up to the Overview's count. Amber, the page strip's "needs a person" colour, is used only for that (at least 4.5:1 as text, 3:1 as a dot, in light and dark mode); source tags (Ship condition, Automated check, Deferred by the AI, Deferred by the executor) are soft grey pills like the item badges, and so is summary.html's "No"; the "Safety" tag is in the page strip's red (a failed safety check means the book may have been damaged), at least 4.5:1 in light and dark mode. When all five safety checks pass, the safety card's "The 5 safety checks" toggle is plain grey; when one fails, it stays a link and its fold opens, failed check first. Status, number, rule-ID, page and header cells don't wrap, other text wraps between words only, and on a narrow screen a table scrolls sideways in its box (which is then a Tab stop); a section with 20 items or more has a search box that hides the rows and list items without the words typed and says how many are shown. It reports WCAG only: veraPDF's PDF/UA-only rules, PDF/UA-only notes (such as a file's own claim of PDF/UA conformance, which is then not counted under "Needs a person") and PDF/UA wording (a WCAG rule whose veraPDF description speaks of PDF/UA is shown by a WCAG name) are left out; review.md keeps them all as the record. One offline file: no internet, no external scripts; it reads in full without JavaScript. Lists are shown in full in both review.md and review.html (the pages with text a screen reader could not reach are written as collapsed ranges, each end linked in review.html; only when even the ranges would be very long, the first 25 pages and "… and N more"). Quoted book text is an excerpt of about 100 characters ending at a word boundary with "…"; review.html has a "show full text" button at the end of each (it opens the whole paragraph in a block below the line and becomes "hide full text"; without JavaScript the whole text is simply shown; a section search that matches only the hidden full text opens it). The whole texts are kept in `record.json` `sections`, `full`. Alt text, the AI's sentences and veraPDF's rule descriptions are never cut. Section badges count real entries only (not notes such as "Not changed: …"). It quotes book text, like review.md, so it stays under `runs/` |
| `record.json` | the archive | Everything vital in one file: source fingerprint, model, prompt version, tokens, cost, the full work order, what was applied, rejected and deferred, the verification results, and Claude's raw reply. It also holds `review.md`'s content as data (`sections`, with `sections_version: 1`): every line of every section as an item with its id, its kind (paragraph, list item, table row), its pages where known, and the parts that only PDF/UA requires. `review.md` and `review.html` are both drawn from it. `apply.py` accepts it in place of a work order, to re-apply a run |
| (review) | the archive | Reviewed runs only: `record.json` adds `review.workorder` (who reviewed the work order, when, and every change from the AI's version), `review.session` (the session this result comes from), `review.sessions` and `review.decisions` (the sessions, and each decision's review status, including the review page's reading-order checks, `reading_order:*`, marked seen with the reviewer's note), `review.run_id` (the run's id from `reviews/basis.json`, also the review page's browser-draft key), `review.run` (the run folder relative to `pipeline/`, for the reopen command), and `workorder_ai` (the AI's version: kept on every reviewed run, whether or not the person changed anything; before 2026-10-27 only when something changed). `review.pending` is set while a later session is finished but not applied (`session`, `status`, `since`, `reviewer`): "Ready to ship?" is then No; it goes when that session is applied. `review.md` adds a checks row and a "Work order reviewed by a person" section. See [Reviewing the work order](review-step.md) |
| `reviews/` | a later review | Every run: `basis.json` (the run id, the input PDF's path and sha256, the digest's fingerprint, the AI order's sha256) and `_audit/` (`digest.json`, 46–355 KB in the 19 test books; `fulltext.json`, the whole texts review.md quotes, local only, 4–186 KB; and the OCR word sheets of a scanned book; never the figure crops), so the run can be reviewed again after code changes. Reviewed runs also: `workorder.ai.json`, `sessions/NNN.json` + `NNN.workorder.json` (each review session and the order it applied) and `versions/<id>/` (each earlier result: PDF, record.json, review.md, review.html, never edited except a "replaced by" note on review.html). The top level always holds the current result. See [Reviewing again](review-step.md#reviewing-again) |
| `han_review.csv` | a Japanese / Chinese reader | Only when the book has Han-only CJK runs: one row per run, with a blank decision column |
| `_intake/intake.json` | the archive | Untagged originals only: what intake measured and did (page triage, text-layer test scores, OCR language, OCR pages, tool versions, every check; `tag_tree` for a placeholder tag tree, with the publisher's alt text it held; `ocr_layer_test` for an existing OCR layer on scanned pages; `spreads` for pages holding two printed pages). Also in `record.json` under `intake` |
| `_intake/intake.md` | a person | Untagged originals only: the intake record in plain words (status, pages OCR'd, picture pages, CHECKs and notes with their pages, the OCR review verdict). Written for every outcome, also by `intake.py <pdf> <dir>` on its own and by `--plan-only` |
| `_intake/intake.pdf` | the pipeline | Untagged originals only, when intake changed anything: the OCR'd and auto-tagged copy the rest of the run worked on. Kept so `FROM=` reruns apply saved replies to the very same file |

The app ([The app](app.md)) keeps its own files in `runs/.app/`, never in a run folder: `jobs/<id>.json` and `<id>.log` (each job: its command, the environment it was given, its status and times, and its output), `dispatch.lock` (the queue's lock), `sha256-cache.json` (sha256s of the PDFs in `samples/`, by path, size and time, for finding a run's original) and `server.log` (errors in the app itself).

A batch folder also holds `summary.md` and `summary.html`: one row per book (ready to ship, needs a person, AI decisions, safety, veraPDF WCAG rules before → after, cost, pages), sorted with the most work first and sortable by any column; each book's name opens its `review.html`. To rebuild the dashboards of a finished run or batch: `python3 report_html.py <run>` or `python3 report_html.py --summary <batch>`.

## `record.json` `run.ocr_words` (scanned books with OCR words)

Only when the audit lists OCR-word forms ([OCR words](ocr-words.md)); other books have just the counts.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `forms`, `sent`, `sheets`, `not_sent`, `not_found_by_audit` | today's audit: forms listed, forms whose word sheet was sent, sheets, forms over the image limit, forms past the audit's cap |
| `fingerprint` | a hash of today's list of sent forms (id and form) |
| `list` | that list itself, `{id: form}` (from 2026-10-27), so a later change could re-match a reply's readings by form |
| `reply_fingerprint`, `reply_read`, `reply_list` | a reused reply only (`REPLY=`, `FROM=`, the regression test): the fingerprint, size and list of the word list the reply was written for, as far as they were recorded with it (none for replies made before the fingerprint; `reply_read` from the earlier record's `sent`) |
| `reply_check` | a reused reply only: `same list` (its readings are used), or `list changed` / `no fingerprint` followed by `: readings dropped` or `: the reply gave no readings` |
| `reply_check_why` | the reason in words, e.g. "the word list changed since (4 forms then, 8 now)"; review.md quotes it |
| `reply_readings_dropped` | how many readings were not used |

## "Overview"

`review.md` opens with an "Overview" block (called "At a glance" in runs made before 2026-10-21), and `_intake/intake.md` with its own. Their numbers are computed once (`lib/glance.py`; for intake, `intake.py`) and kept in `record.json` as `glance` (intake's in `intake.json`, and so in `record.json` under `intake`), so review.md, review.html and summary.html always agree.

- **Ready to ship?** Yes only when the work order was approved on the review page (`REVIEW=1`; see [Sign-off](review-step.md#sign-off-when-a-book-is-ready-to-ship)), the OCR text was approved when intake added OCR text or found the book's existing OCR layer doubtful, unusable or missing diacritics, or could not check it because no sample page could be scored (decided 2026-10-25: an unknown layer is not "not needed"), and the safety checks are OK. "Manual check (PAC)" is listed for information: PAC 2026 is the final manual check after it. A book whose text layer is the publisher's own OCR, checked by that test and not flagged, shows "not needed", with a pointer to the review.md section about it.
- **Safety checks:** pages look the same, text unchanged, all content tagged or decoration, the tag-tree index points into the tree, every font readable. "OK ✔" or "FAILED ✘", never colour alone.
- **Needs a person** = the AI's deferrals + the executor's deferrals (counted by kind and page: 13 captions on 4 pages count 4; all Han-only runs count 1) + CHECK rows, leaving out "Nothing deferred by the executor", which only points at that list. One item can be counted twice when a CHECK row and a deferral describe the same failure (e.g. veraPDF 7.1-3 and the AI's deferral about it).
- **AI decisions:** every entry in the work order (deferrals and the note to the reviewer are not decisions); "approved by NAME on DATE" once the review page has approved it.
- **Fixed automatically (no AI):** the executor's own fixes, by kind (links tied and described, note IDs, tag-tree index entries, standard tags un-remapped, empty tags, decorative lines, tab order, font maps, …), with no total: several touch the same link.
- **Top issues:** safety failures first, then the ship conditions (OCR text, the review), CHECK rows, the executor's deferrals and the AI's; review.md shows five and "… and N more", the dashboard all of them. Each points to its review.md section.
- **intake.md:** the result, what needs a person, the OCR verdict, and "Is the existing text trustworthy?": how many sample pages were read by OCR and compared with the PDF's own text, the typical (median) match, the best and worst pages, and the pages over 5% and 15%, with how to read the numbers.

`review_ocr.py` refreshes all of it when it records a verdict, since "Ready to ship?" depends on it: it changes the OCR row in `record.json`'s `sections`, recomputes the glance, and draws `review.md` and `review.html` again. For a run made before `sections` existed (no `sections_version`), it edits `review.md`'s text instead, and `report_html.py` reads `review.md` to build the dashboard.

Nothing else is kept. The working files (digest, figure crops, raw request and response, logs, and with `REVIEW=1` the review's page images, OCR word crops and files) are deleted once the report is written, because they are rebuilt from the original in under a minute and the crops are copies of book pages. A run that fails partway keeps its working files, so the failure can be diagnosed.
